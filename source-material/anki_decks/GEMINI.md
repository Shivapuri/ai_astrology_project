# Anki Flashcard Strategy & Guidelines (Vedic Astrology)

## Core Philosophy: Minimum Information Principle & Atomization
Astrology is multidimensional: each planet, house, and sign has dozens of significations (*karakatwas*). Memorizing a giant bulleted list of 8–10 disparate items on a single card violates the **Minimum Information Principle** (from SuperMemo's 20 Rules of Formulating Knowledge). It leads to "Anki purgatory" (forgetting one item out of ten and failing the card repeatedly).

To achieve 90%+ retention without cognitive fatigue, all broad astrological concepts must be systematically structured.

---

## 1. The Rule of 4 (Atomization)
Never put a long, mixed list of attributes on a single card. Every Planet, House, and Sign must consistently be broken into **exactly four separate sub-cards** based on natural astrological categories:

1. **Card 1: Core / Psychological Themes**
   - *Prompt:* What are the **core and psychological themes** of [Entity]?
   - *Scope:* Mind, emotional temperament, primary motivation, behavioral tendencies, Dharma.
   - *Example (Jupiter):* Innate wisdom, higher knowledge, optimism, faith, spirituality, and grace.

2. **Card 2: Physical / Biological**
   - *Prompt:* What **physical body parts, organs, and biological functions** does [Entity] govern?
   - *Scope:* Organs, tissues (*dhatus*), vitality, physical vulnerabilities.
   - *Example (Jupiter):* The brain, liver, and adipose (fat) tissue.

3. **Card 3: People & Relational**
   - *Prompt:* Which **people, family members, and societal roles** are represented by [Entity]?
   - *Scope:* Relatives, social archetypes, professional roles.
   - *Example (Jupiter):* Children, elder brothers, the Guru, teachers, and counselors.

4. **Card 4: Material & Environmental**
   - *Prompt:* What **material objects, places, and external environments** are associated with [Entity]?
   - *Scope:* Careers, external objects, physical settings, financial manifestations.
   - *Example (Jupiter):* Luck, wealth, religious institutions, courts, and law.

---

## 2. No Trivial "Reverse" Guessing
Avoid basic reverse cards where the prompt immediately gives away the answer:
- ❌ *Bad:* Front: "Which planet is the wise Guru?" -> Back: "Jupiter" *(Too easy; the prompt does the thinking).*
- ✅ *Good:* Testing must flow from the astrological symbol to its concrete manifestation:
  - *Front:* "Which societal roles and family members does **Jupiter** signify?"
  - *Back:* "Children, elder brothers, teachers, and counselors."

*Note:* Diagnostic synthesis questions (e.g., "Which houses form the Trines?" or "Which planets gain Digbala in the 10th house?") are acceptable because they test structural chart geometry, not trivial keyword matching.

---

## 3. Mnemonic Isolation
- If a mnemonic phrase or acronym is used to remember an entity's pillars, **isolate it onto its own card**.
- ❌ *Bad:* Bundling the mnemonic at the bottom of a list of 10 attributes.
- ✅ *Good:* Front: "What is the mnemonic phrase for **Jupiter**?" -> Back: *"The WISE GURU teaches you to see BIG, have FAITH, FORGIVE, and GROW."*

---

## 4. Question Phrasing & HTML Styling
- Use natural, grammatically complete English sentences on the front (avoid robotic phrases like *"What are the Physical/Biological of Moon?"*).
- Highlight key terms with `<b>` bold tags.
- Use `<ul><li>` bullet points for lists of 2–4 items to maintain fast visual scannability.
- Every card should be answerable within **3 to 5 seconds**.
